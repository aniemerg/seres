# Addendum: Notes on questionable equations & lunar-appropriate fixes

This reaction list mixes (a) balanced “net reactions,” (b) shorthand process arrows, and (c) geochemical/hydrothermal-style transformations. A few entries are **chemically ambiguous** (mass balance issues) or **missing required reactants** (oxidants/reductants), which matters if you plan to simulate mass/energy closure or build a process graph.

Below are the issues identified, why they matter in a lunar setting, and suggested fixes.

## General lunar context reminders (so we don’t accidentally import Earth assumptions)

* The Moon provides **no atmosphere** to supply O₂, CO₂, or H₂O at scale. Any O₂/CO₂/H₂O used in reactions must come from **(i) volatiles extraction**, **(ii) electrolysis**, **(iii) carbon/hydrogen import**, or **(iv) closed-loop recycling**.
* Liquid water is generally an **engineered phase**: a sealed pressurized reactor, transient melt, or ice processing loop. If a reaction uses aqueous chemistry (HCl leaching, clay formation, etc.), assume **contained** reactors and account for water recovery.
* Many “alteration” reactions (serpentinization/clay formation) are **Earth-geology pathways**. They can still be valid *chemically* on the Moon in engineered hydrothermal reactors, but are not “ambient lunar” processes.

## 1) Selenite → elemental selenium line is missing a reductant

**Problem line (from list):**
`Na2SeO3 + H2SO4 → Na2O + H2SO4 + Se`  (and labeled “photosensitive Se”)

### Why it’s an issue

* H₂SO₄ appears on both sides unchanged; oxygen/hydrogen balance is off.
* Making **Se(0)** from **selenite (Se(IV))** requires a **reducing agent**. Acid alone does not reduce selenium.

### Lunar-appropriate fix: split into explicit steps

Treat this as a **two-stage block**: (A) acidification, (B) reduction.

**A. Acidification (makes selenous acid):**
`Na2SeO3 + H2SO4 → H2SeO3 + Na2SO4`

**B. Reduction to elemental Se (choose a reductant you can plausibly source on the Moon):**
Pick one depending on your plant design:

* If you have **H₂** (common in many lunar flows):
  `H2SeO3 + 2H2 → Se + 3H2O`

* If you have **SO₂** (you do, from troilite oxidation):
  `H2SeO3 + 2SO2 + H2O → Se + 2H2SO4`
  (Nice because it regenerates H₂SO₄, closing an acid loop.)

**Agent instruction:** replace the single “selenite→Se” line with the 2-step block and explicitly choose a reductant based on available upstream products (H₂ vs SO₂ etc.).

## 2) Fayalite → magnetite equation cancels water and likely isn’t balanced

**Problem line (from list):**
`3Fe2SiO4 + 2H2O → 2Fe3O4 + 3SiO2 + 2H2O`

### Why it’s an issue

* Water appears on both sides in equal amounts → it cancels and does no chemical work.
* Converting Fe²⁺ silicate (fayalite) to mixed Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ oxide (magnetite) requires an **oxidant** (typically O₂) or an explicit redox partner.

### Lunar-appropriate fix: treat as “conceptual” unless oxidant is specified

Two ways to repair depending on intended process:

**Option A: Explicit oxidation using O₂ you produce**
If you have O₂ from water electrolysis or oxide electrolysis, write it explicitly. (The exact balanced form depends on whether you go to magnetite vs hematite and how silica is handled; don’t hardcode a questionable balance.)

**Option B: Hydrothermal alteration path**
If the intent is a hydrothermal mineral transformation (engineered reactor with water), then products typically contain hydroxyl-bearing phases (serpentine/brucite), not just SiO₂. Again, don’t keep the “water cancels” version.

**Agent instruction:** keep a note: “fayalite can be converted toward magnetite/silica under oxidizing or hydrothermal conditions,” but require the agent to (i) pick an oxidant source (O₂, ferric oxide, etc.) and (ii) re-balance before using in mass-closure simulation.

## 3) Forsterite “hydration” to MgO + SiO₂ cancels water; likely wrong mechanism

**Problem line (from list):**
`Mg2SiO4 + 4H2O → 2MgO + SiO2 + 4H2O`  (for Sorel cement)

### Why it’s an issue

* Again water cancels; the implied net reaction is `Mg2SiO4 → 2MgO + SiO2`, which is not a typical low-temp aqueous transformation.
* If the goal is **MgO** for Sorel cement, the most credible lunar routes are:

  * **High-temperature reduction / decomposition** sequences (carbothermal, methane reduction, electrolysis)
  * **Acid leach + precipitation/calcination** sequences
  * **Electrochemical extraction** from molten salts/oxides

### Lunar-appropriate fixes (choose based on your plant)

* If you already have the high-temp methane route listed (`Mg2SiO4 + 2CH4 → ... + 2MgO + Si ...`), then treat MgO as coming from that pathway, not “hydration.”
* If you want an aqueous route, represent it as **leach → precipitate → calcine** (explicitly showing where heat and water separation happen).

**Agent instruction:** do not use the “hydration → MgO” equation as a balanced reaction. Replace with either the existing high-T reduction route or a multi-step leach/precip/calcine block.

## 4) Ostwald nitric acid step is OK, but it hides NO₂ intermediate

**Problem line (from list):**
`3NO + H2O → 2HNO3 + NO`  (labeled Ostwald)

### Why it can confuse an agent

* This is effectively the absorption step but the chemically relevant intermediate is **NO₂**; omitting it can break redox bookkeeping if the agent tries to close O₂ flows.

### Lunar-appropriate fix: expand to the standard two-step

* `2NO + O2 → 2NO2`
* `3NO2 + H2O → 2HNO3 + NO`

**Agent instruction:** if building a process graph, use the two-step form so O₂ requirements and NO recycle are explicit. (O₂ must be sourced from your O₂ production loop, not “air.”)

## 5) Sodium silicate equilibrium does not directly “make piezoelectric quartz”

**Problem line (from list):**
`Na2CO3 + SiO2(l) ↔ Na2SiO3 + CO2 → piezoelectric quartz`
(with “40–80 day growth” noted)

### Why it’s an issue

* The equilibrium is for **sodium silicate formation / silica transport chemistry**; quartz growth is a **separate precipitation/crystallization process**.
* On the Moon, if you’re growing quartz intentionally, you’ll likely do it in a **contained thermal gradient** or controlled precipitation system. The reaction is part of a solvent/transport loop, not the final “quartz reaction.”

### Lunar-appropriate fix: separate “transport medium” from “crystallization step”

* Keep: `Na2CO3 + SiO2 ↔ Na2SiO3 + CO2` as **silica dissolution/transport** (requires a CO₂ source or a closed carbonate loop).
* Add a separate block: “Quartz crystallization (controlled precipitation / thermal gradient) → SiO₂(s, quartz)” with growth time as an operational parameter, not stoichiometry.

**Agent instruction:** tag the sodium silicate equilibrium as “silica transport / precursor,” and model quartz formation as a downstream unit operation.

## 6) General guidance for simulation vs “process notes”

For any line that:

* has a reactant on both sides unchanged (e.g., H₂SO₄, H₂O),
* implies oxidation/reduction without specifying oxidant/reductant,
* or labels a long multi-day crystallization step as a direct product,

…treat it as **process shorthand**, not a final balanced reaction. Convert it into:

1. balanced sub-reactions, and/or
2. unit operations (leach, separation, calcination, electrolysis, crystallization), with explicit inputs/outputs.
